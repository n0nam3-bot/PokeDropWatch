POKÉDROP WATCH GUIDE

How the monitor works

PokéDrop Watch checks configured retailer and news pages on a schedule, looks for Pokémon-related products or articles, compares the results with its saved scan history, and publishes the changes it can verify. A status describes what the automated request and parser could see; it is not a guarantee that a store has stock.

What source status means

StatusWhat happenedHow to interpret it
MATCHESThe page/feed responded and the parser recognized one or more usable Pokémon entries.At least one listing was read. It does not mean every listing was found or that every item is available to buy.
NO MATCHThe page responded, but the parser found zero usable Pokémon entries.The retailer may still show products in a normal browser. The page may render products with JavaScript, use a changed layout, or provide data the current parser does not recognize.
ISSUEThe request failed before a usable page could be parsed, such as a timeout, connection error, block, DNS/TLS error, or HTTP failure.This is an automated-access problem. It does not prove the retailer's public website is unavailable to people.

When several URLs are configured for one source, the dashboard reports the overall result and how many configured pages responded. A successful alternate URL can still provide listings when the primary URL fails.

How a scan becomes an alert

  1. Fetch: the scheduled GitHub Actions monitor requests each configured source URL and any configured alternatives.
  2. Parse: retailer-specific and shared parsers look for product titles, links, images, prices, release dates, and stock indicators.
  3. Normalize: product links and titles are standardized so duplicate pages or tracking URLs are less likely to create duplicate listings.
  4. Compare: the current catalog is compared with saved product and stock history to identify meaningful changes.
  5. Publish and notify: the latest status and alert events are written to the dashboard data. Matching events are eligible for delivery to approved, active subscribers.

The monitor can only report information exposed to its automated request. A normal browser may show additional results because it runs page scripts, uses a logged-in session, or receives a different version of the page.

What the alert filters mean

FilterMeaningWhen it appears
New itemsA listing has a published release date within 30 days before or after today.The listing has a parseable release date inside the 30-day window.
First seenThe monitor discovered the listing, but its release date is outside that window or unavailable.A newly discovered listing does not qualify as a dated New item.
RestocksA listing changed from an observed out-of-stock state to an observed in-stock state.Both stock states must have been observed. A listing that merely appears for the first time is not a restock.
Price dropsA comparable current price is lower than its previously saved price.The monitor has both a previous and a current price to compare.
NewsA Pokémon news article was recognized from a configured news feed/source.The feed provides an article entry the news parser can read.

Product filters

Item type narrows listings to TCG, plush, collectibles, games, accessories and apparel, or news. Retailer narrows products to Amazon, Walmart, Best Buy, Target, Pokémon Center, or GameStop. Exact title requires the search phrase to match the normalized title instead of matching part of it.

All tracked products shows the latest catalog listings, not only event history. Latest activity shows detected events such as first discoveries, restocks, price changes, and news.

How email notifications work

  1. A visitor selects the categories and retailers/sources they want to follow and requests a verification code.
  2. The six-digit code is sent to the entered mailbox. It expires after 10 minutes and can only be used once.
  3. After successful verification, the application is saved with Pending status.
  4. An administrator reviews the application. Only Active subscribers are eligible to receive matching alert emails.
  5. Subscriber preferences are checked against each event before the email is sent. Unsubscribe requests also require a one-time email code.

Approval is separate from email verification: verifying an email address does not automatically activate a subscription.

Why a product may have missing details

  • No image: the page may not expose a usable product image, may return a placeholder, or may block external image loading.
  • No price: the source may hide the price until a page script runs, require a location/session, or expose a price format the parser cannot interpret.
  • Unknown availability: no reliable in-stock or out-of-stock indicator was found. The dashboard does not guess.
  • Missing release date: the retailer did not publish a date in the structured data or product markup the monitor received.

Source layouts and access rules can change. A parser miss should be investigated with the corresponding scan log and a sanitized response sample rather than treated as proof that no product exists.
